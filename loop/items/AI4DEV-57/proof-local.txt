AN INDEPENDENT OBSERVATION MADE ALONGSIDE THIS RUN, BECAUSE (f2) ALONE REPORTS A BOOLEAN AND NOT A
VALUE. Check (f2) compares the redirect's client_id against the SCRIPT's own environment, so it

This matters because of what happened before the restart, recorded here as the diagnostic it is.
The founder attempted a real Google sign-in against the stack as it was then running and Google
answered 401 invalid_client. The cause was that the stack had been started with the variables absent
from its environment, and THE SUPABASE CLI DOES NOT SUBSTITUTE AN EMPTY VALUE IN THAT CASE - IT
PASSES THE LITERAL STRING "env(SUPABASE_AUTH_EXTERNAL_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID)" THROUGH AS THE CLIENT ID.
That is CLI behaviour and not a defect in the committed configuration: config.toml uses Supabase's
own documented env() syntax and is correct as written. The observation above is what says the
substitution is now genuinely happening.

THE 401 IS ALSO THE SHARPEST AVAILABLE PROOF OF WHAT CHECK (f) NEVER ESTABLISHED. On that broken
stack, /auth/v1/settings still reported google=true and (f) still passed, while the configured
client id was a meaningless literal. So (f) proves the provider block is well-formed and enabled,
and proves NOTHING about whether the client id means anything. The record already said exactly that
on reasoning alone; this is the measurement behind it.
