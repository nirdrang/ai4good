Why this file exists
--------------------
`tests/at/expected/req-001.json` declares 21 of REQ-001's 37 P0 ids red, and
`tests/at/harness/expected.ts` matches such a red by SHAPE: it rebuilds the anchored prefix

    AtPending: <id> PENDING [sut-missing] -

and compares it from position 0. Everything AFTER the em dash is free. So a stub whose detail
read "todo" would satisfy `at:check`, `at:verify --expect` and CI alike, while the record said
nothing about who lands the id or when.

Nothing mechanical closes that. This file is the check that does: one line per pending id, and
every deliverable-and-leaf named below really appears in `loop/decomp/req-001.md` at revision
0579425. A reviewer compares the two files. No per-stub oracle is built - that is harness work
and belongs to the harness item, not to a leaf.

The detail strings themselves live in `tests/at/suites/req-001/_pending.ts` (the `LEAF` map) and
are attached to each id at its own `atTest` call site.

Two things this ledger does NOT claim
-------------------------------------
That a later leaf will keep its promise. The ledger names the leaf; it cannot make that leaf land
the id. What it does is make a broken promise VISIBLE - if a leaf lands and the id stays red, the
two files disagree and a reader can see which one is lying.

And, specific to the three ids this leaf removed:

  * AT-001.19's green at INTEGRATION tier is the email/Google completion path only. A
    github-established session needs a real consent round trip, which is why AT-001.02, .04 and
    .05 are all capability-pending at that tier; the volunteer route through a linked GitHub
    identity is proved at LOOP tier, against the same shared validation the deployed function
    runs. The narrowing is stated in the plan, in the integration body itself, and in the merge
    ruling.
  * AT-001.20 claims the CONTENT of the shipped copy and never its display. No screen exists in
    this tree. The copy is a shipped constant module that `validateCompleteSignup` imports, so
    its authority statement has behavioural force at run time - the server refuses any attestation
    that is not that statement - but the prohibition and the recommendation are graded as words,
    by direct import, and a green over them says nothing about any surface showing them to
    anybody.
  * AT-001.39 proves that a refusal writes NOTHING - no account, no organisation, no membership,
    no acknowledgment, and the platform-acknowledgment predicate still false. It does not prove
    mid-transaction rollback: the refusal comes from the shared validation before the database is
    called, so zero rows survive because none was ever written. The database's own `not null` and
    whitespace constraints are the floor under a caller that never went through the edge function,
    and that floor is structural rather than exercised by these bodies.

None of the three says anything about future acknowledgment moments. Funding acknowledgments and
REQ-006's will reuse `public.acknowledgments`, whose constraints now force the three fields; that
constraint is the hook they inherit, not a claim proved here.
